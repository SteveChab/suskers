# The Man Who Was Someone Else

I woke up one morning and decided to become a better person. I bought a seven-dollar pen because I always lose pens and I got sick of not caring. Then I used it to write a list of goals, which included learning to play tennis, traveling the world, and becoming good at everything.

I used to play sports. Then I realized you can buy trophies. Now I am good at everything.

First, I needed a map of the world. I wanted to hang it in my house and put pins into all the locations I had traveled to. But first, I had to travel to the top two corners of the map so it wouldn't fall down.

I figured I might as well start locally. I walked down the street, where a guy waved at me. He came up and said, "I'm sorry, I thought you were someone else."

"I am," I said.

He seemed confused, so I left him there and continued toward the train station. On the way, I passed a picket line. I'm against picketing, but I don't know how to show it. I considered holding up a sign that said so, but that would make me a picketer, which would defeat the purpose.

I decided to take the escalator instead. I like escalators because an escalator can never break. It can only become stairs. There would never be an "Escalator Temporarily Out Of Order" sign, only "Escalator Temporarily Stairs. Sorry for the convenience."

At the bottom, I saw a forklift lifting a crate of forks. It was way too literal for me, so I went to a bookstore to escape reality.

If I had a bookstore, I would make all the mystery novels hard to find. Unfortunately, the store I visited had a sign that said EVERY BOOK IS A CHILDREN'S BOOK IF THE KID CAN READ, which I thought was a little too permissive. I picked up a book about bravery, courage, valor, and daring. I soon discovered the author was holding a thesaurus.

I bought the book anyway. Then I went to a restaurant for lunch.

The restaurant had a sign that said "Restrooms For Customers Only." I thought, it must suck to work there.

I ordered fettuccine Alfredo because it's macaroni and cheese for adults. I also ordered rice, because rice is great if you're really hungry and want to eat 2,000 of something. For dessert, the waiter offered me a frozen banana.

"No, but I want a regular banana later, so... yeah."

He looked at me like I was a know-it-all. A know-it-all is a person who knows everything except for how annoying he is.

I tipped him with a fruit basket. I like fruit baskets because they give you the ability to mail someone a piece of fruit without appearing insane. My favorite fruit is grapes, because with grapes, you always get another chance.

After lunch, I went to the casino. I'm not addicted to gambling. I'm addicted to sitting in a semi-circle. I sat down at the table and played until I lost everything except my seven-dollar pen and my map.

I told the dealer I could stop any time. I knew a guy who's addicted to brake fluid, but he says he can stop any time, so I figured we had something in common.

On the way out, I passed a DJ Abraham Lincoln impersonator. It's an easy way to make someone sound less powerful, just put DJ in front of their name. DJ Abraham Lincoln.

He was signing autographs, and I asked him to sign my cast. I'd broken my arm earlier that week in a tennis match.

The easiest time to add insult to injury is when you're signing somebody's cast. He wrote, "Nice arm. Shame about your game."

I thanked him and went home.

My house was a disaster. My fake plants had died because I did not pretend to water them. My dog sat in the middle of the living room, forever in the push-up position. I decided to get a girlfriend so I would have someone to help me clean.

I don't have a girlfriend. But I do know a woman who'd be mad at me for saying that.

She arrived that evening. We watched a movie, and I held her hand. I like to hold hands at the movies, which always seems to startle strangers. The couple next to us looked horrified, so we held hands even harder.

Afterward, we went outside and saw a bird fly into a sliding glass door. Then a bee flew into it. Then a running child did the same.

The bird, the bee, the running child are all the same to the sliding glass door.

My girlfriend suggested we go on vacation. I agreed, but I needed to finish my jigsaw puzzle first. I'd bought a 40,000-piece puzzle that, when completed, would say "go outside."

We worked on it for three days. On the fourth day, I gave up and went outside.

That's when I saw Bigfoot.

He was standing beside a tennis court, but he was blurry. That's the problem with Bigfoot. It's not the photographer's fault. Bigfoot is blurry, and that's extra scary to me.

I tried to take a picture, but my camera wouldn't focus. I decided to challenge him to tennis instead. The depressing thing about tennis is that no matter how much I play, I'll never be as good as a wall.

Bigfoot beat me six-love.

I was devastated. My girlfriend tried to comfort me by saying, "I'm sorry."

I told her that saying "I'm sorry" is the same as saying "I apologize."

Except at a funeral.

She left me.

I spent the next two weeks trying to figure out what had gone wrong. I didn't tell anyone when I finally realized the answer, because I like to wait two weeks to tell people when good things happen to me. I like to use the word "fortnight."

During that fortnight, I started a new hobby. I bought a jackhammer.

I think one of the most groundbreaking inventions of all time is the jackhammer.

I used it to dig a hole in my backyard, where I buried my trophies. I was tired of being good at everything. I wanted to be bad at something, just to feel alive.

I also started a band. We played one show at a funeral, where I had a heart attack in the middle of a game of charades. The paramedics arrived just as I was acting out "elephant." They couldn't tell if I was dying or performing.

I survived, but the doctor told me I needed to wear a vest. I asked whether a sweater vest would protect me from pretty girls.

He said no.

I bought one anyway.

Then I went home and ate breakfast. I like to use "I Can't Believe It's Not Butter" on my toast in the morning, because sometimes when I eat breakfast, I like to be incredulous.

After breakfast, I opened my computer. Whenever I'm on my computer, I don't type "lol." I type "lqtm." Laugh quietly to myself. It's more honest.

I typed a message to my former girlfriend. I apologized for everything, then deleted it. I didn't want to seem desperate.

Instead, I sent her a frozen banana.

She called me immediately.

"Why did you send me a frozen banana?"

"Because I want a regular banana later," I said.

She hung up.

I decided to move on. I started a new business selling books, trophies, and fruit baskets. I also opened a casino, where every game involved sitting in a semi-circle. I called it The Fortnight.

Business was terrible, so I hired a marketing consultant. He told me to become an influencer. I told him I didn't believe in influencing people.

He asked me what I believed in.

"I am bravery. I am courage. I am valor. I am daring."

He stared at me.

"I'm holding a thesaurus," I explained.

He quit.

I was back where I started: alone, with a dead houseplant, a broken jackhammer, and a map that still wouldn't stay on the wall.

So I bought a dog. I liked dogs because they're forever in the push-up position, and I thought one might help me get in shape.

I also bought a frozen banana.

Then I sat down at the kitchen table and wrote a new list of goals with my seven-dollar pen. I would find a girlfriend, win a tennis match, travel the world, and discover why Bigfoot was blurry.

I put on my sweater vest, grabbed my map, and headed for the door.

I walked straight into the sliding glass door.

The bird, the bee, the running child, and I.

We all had something in common.

And for the first time in my life, I felt like I belonged somewhere.

I picked up my map, pinned it to the wall, and sat down to eat rice.

Two thousand grains at a time.

Then I heard a knock at the door.

It was the guy from the street.

"I'm sorry," he said. "I thought you were someone else."

I smiled.

"I am."

He nodded, as though this explained everything.

Then he handed me a book.

It was a mystery novel.

I couldn't find it.
